# WP-08 in plain words: the gauntlet

- The grey stand-in hand is gone. In its place is a full steel gauntlet: a forearm guard (vambrace) with a strap and buckle, a flared cuff, three sliding wrist plates, a plate over the back of the hand, a knuckle plate with four bumps, and every finger and the thumb made of overlapping little plates (lames) like real armour.
- Every plate has a rolled edge (a tiny bead along its border, like real armour metal folded over), and there are 60 small domed rivets.
- Under the plates there is dark leather, which you see in the gaps when the fingers bend.
- The index fingertip has a little hinged cap (a "visor"). When it opens you see a glowing ember socket — that is where the spark lives (WP-09).
- It has a skeleton of 19 bones, each with limits so a finger can never bend the wrong way, and seven hand shapes (poses) it can blend between smoothly: hover, point, ready-to-pinch, pinch, stretch, release, receive.
- A test checks 66 different hand shapes and wrist angles and makes sure no plate ever pokes through another.
- The steel is polished and reflects the warm paper and the walnut board; only its brightest glints glow (bloom). Its shadow falls on the paper and on the printed words in the same way.
- Budget: about 149,000 triangles on good computers, about 53,000 on the "Low" setting; it costs about 1.5–2.4 ms per frame on Paul's Mac.
- Try it: open `valheim-checkup.html?gauntlet` to see it on a turntable — buttons for close/medium/far, every pose, the light version (LOD1) and a wireframe view.
- Files: `src/gauntlet/` (model.js builds it, shell.js makes the plates, rig.js the skeleton and arm, poses.js the hand shapes, collide.js the "no poking through" check, review.js the turntable page).
