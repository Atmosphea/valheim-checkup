# WP-04 · The 3D desk's foundation, in plain language

## What was built

The page can now open as a **3D desk** instead of the flat page. For now the desk holds stand-in shapes: wooden planks, a cream sheet of paper, a smaller sheet on top of it, a brown box where the carved woodblock will go, five flat gold discs where the coins will go, and a shiny steel tube with a ball on the end, standing in for the gauntlet. The real models come later (the woodblock and coins in WP-06, the gauntlet in WP-08), and so do the real looks (paper fibres, oiled wood, brushed steel in WP-05).

What *is* real already is the machinery around them:

- **The camera.** Like a long lens on a tripod: a narrow 28° view, looking down at the desk at 62°, set back just far enough that the paper fills the window like Paul's reference picture. When you move the mouse, the camera drifts a little toward it, up to 1.6 cm sideways and 1 cm up or down, and zooms in 2.5% when the mouse is near the middle. It follows on a spring, so it glides instead of jumping.
- **The lights.** A warm lamp from the upper left (the colour of a 3,200-kelvin bulb, like old tungsten light) that casts soft shadows; a dim warm "sky" light for fill; a small cool light from the right to catch steel edges; and a spark light that WP-09 will move around. Shiny things reflect a little pretend photo studio built inside the scene: one big warm softbox, one small cool panel and one bright strip.
- **The seven layers.** The desk is made of seven see-through sheets stacked on top of each other, like animation cels:

  | # | Layer | What it holds |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1 | `#scene` | The 3D picture (desk, paper, board, coins, gauntlet) |
  | 2 | `#print` | Real HTML text, tilted in 3D so it lies exactly on the paper |
  | 3 | `#veil` | The light and shadow on the paper, *multiplied* over layers 1 and 2 |
  | 4 | `#glow` | Glows that brighten what is under them (the spark's light pool later) |
  | 5 | `#float` | Things that float above the paper (Details sheet, menus, toasts) |
  | 6 | `#fx` | The spark and its sparkles (WP-09) |
  | 7 | `#grain` | Film grain (10%) and darker corners (18%) over everything |

- **Quality levels.** High, Medium and Low. When the page opens it times 60 frames and picks the level the computer can handle. If it later gets slow (frames taking over 20 ms for 2 seconds), it drops one level and never climbs back up by itself.
- **Mode choice.** The desk needs WebGL2 (the browser's 3D drawing system), a mouse or trackpad, no "reduce motion" setting, and a window of at least 1024 × 640. Otherwise you get the flat page, exactly as before.

## How it works

- **Centimetres everywhere.** One unit in the 3D world is one centimetre. The middle of the main sheet is (0, 0); x goes right, y goes up the page, and z comes out of the paper towards you. The sheet runs from −48 to 48 across and −27 to 27 up and down: 96 × 54 cm, the same shape as a widescreen TV.
- **Why text lies perfectly on the paper.** Layer 1 and layer 2 use the *same* camera. Layer 2 uses a tool called `CSS3DRenderer`, which turns the camera into a CSS 3D transform, so the browser tilts and shrinks real HTML exactly like the 3D paper. The text is written twice as large as it appears and then shrunk, so it stays sharp when the camera zooms.
- **Why text gets the same light as the paper.** Layer 1 draws the paper with no lighting at all: just its colour. Layer 3 draws the lighting by itself (white where it's bright, grey in shadow) and is *multiplied* over everything below. Multiplying by white changes nothing; multiplying by grey darkens. Because the text in layer 2 sits *under* layer 3, a shadow darkens the paper and the ink by exactly the same amount.
- **Glows work the other way.** Layer 4 uses "screen" blending, which can only brighten. A warm glow there lifts both the paper and the ink.
- **One heartbeat.** Every frame, one function runs these steps in order: read the mouse → move the springs and the camera → draw the 3D scene → draw the veil → draw glows and grain → move the printed text. Later work packages hook into these steps instead of starting their own timers. When the tab is hidden, the heartbeat skips drawing.
- **Hover by "raycasting".** To know what the mouse is over, the code shoots an invisible ray from the camera through the mouse into the scene and sees what it hits first. That is only used to make things react when you hover. Clicks and the keyboard go to real, invisible HTML buttons in layer 2, so screen readers and the Tab key keep working.

## One thing to try

Build and open the desk, then open the browser's console (in Chrome: View → Developer → JavaScript Console) and type:

```js
__vc.stage.test.printDiv(); __vc.stage.test.shadow({ x: 4, y: 7, r: 15, height: 20 })
```

You'll see a line of text appear on the smaller sheet and a soft shadow fall across it. The letters get darker inside the shadow, just like the paper. Then try `__vc.stage.test.glow({ x: 4, y: 7, r: 16 })` for a warm glow, and `__vc.stage.test.clear()` to tidy up. Move the mouse around slowly and watch the camera drift after it. Type `__vc.quality('low')` and `__vc.quality('high')` to compare the quality levels.
